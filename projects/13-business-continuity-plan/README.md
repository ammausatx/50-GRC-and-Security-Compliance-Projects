# Project 13: Develop a Business Continuity Plan

**Level:** Beginner | **Track:** Governance, Risk, and Compliance

## Objective
Create a practical, repeatable develop a business continuity plan workflow for a fictional organization. Demonstrate governance ownership, risk-based decisions, traceability, and evidence-backed conclusions.

## Scope and authorization
Use a fictional company, synthetic records, and authorized test environments only. Define organizational boundary, business processes, systems, data types, third parties, exclusions, assumptions, and review period. Do not publish real personal, regulated, customer, or confidential information. This training is not legal advice, certification, or an audit opinion.

## Prerequisites
- Spreadsheet/GRC workspace, document editor, and diagramming tool
- Fictional sponsor, process owner, control owner, reviewer, and risk approver
- Current official framework or regulatory source material
- Synthetic sample data and unique record identifiers

## Lab setup
1. Create folders for scope, requirements, assessment, evidence, remediation, and reporting.
2. Document purpose, boundaries, stakeholders, assets/processes, dependencies, exclusions, and assumptions.
3. Establish IDs for requirements, controls, risks, evidence, findings, and corrective actions.
4. Define scoring, status values, sampling, review, and approval rules before testing.
5. Version the artifacts and record source editions and assessment date.

## Walkthrough
### 1. Plan and authorize
Write a charter with business objective, scope, roles, responsibilities, milestones, assumptions, and exclusions. Obtain sponsor approval before assessment work.

### 2. Establish requirements
Translate the topic into testable criteria. Record source and exact reference, applicability rationale, expected state, owner, procedure, evidence needed, and review frequency. Separate binding obligations from voluntary guidance.

### 3. Build registers
Create linked requirements, controls, risks, evidence, findings, and action registers. Include unique ID, owner, status, evidence reference, assessment date, risk rating, due date, reviewer, and approval.

### 4. Collect and test evidence
Interview the fictional owner, inspect synthetic records, and sample representative cases. Record population, selection method, sample size, procedure, observed facts, exceptions, date, and evidence location. Mark unsupported statements unable to verify.

### 5. Evaluate results
Compare control design and operation with each criterion. Document condition, expected state, cause, impact, likelihood, severity, scope, and confidence. Do not infer operating effectiveness from policy text alone.

### 6. Manage gaps
Choose mitigation, avoidance, transfer/share, or risk acceptance. Assign an accountable owner, milestones, target date, interim safeguards, and residual-risk approver. Exceptions require rationale, compensating safeguards, expiry, and periodic review.

### 7. Communicate and retain
Prepare an executive summary, methodology, results, evidence index, prioritized actions, decisions, limitations, and next review date. Retain protected evidence in a controlled repository; keep this public project synthetic.

## Tools and checks
Use spreadsheets with validation lists, unique IDs, filters, pivot summaries, and overdue-action formulas; use document version history and approval records. Optional scripts may flag duplicate IDs, missing owners, incomplete evidence references, and overdue actions. Only use synthetic or read-only fixtures.

## Expected output
A reproducible assessment pack containing charter, scope, criteria, registers, sample tests, evidence index, findings, risk decisions, corrective actions, approvals, and executive report.

## Findings and risk assessment
Classify outcomes as effective, partially effective, ineffective, not implemented, not applicable with rationale, or unable to verify. Prioritize by likelihood, impact, exposure, and applicable contractual/regulatory significance. Distinguish design gaps from operating failures.

## Framework mapping
Record the exact framework edition and clause/control references applicable to this project. Depending on context, consider ISO/IEC 27001:2022, NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, CIS Controls v8, SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria, and relevant sector requirements. Crosswalks are informative and do not guarantee equivalence.

## Remediation
For every confirmed gap, document root cause, corrective action, owner, priority, dependencies, interim controls, due date, closure evidence, and independent validation. Escalate overdue high-risk issues through governance.

## Validation criteria
- Scope, criteria, and methodology are approved and versioned.
- Every conclusion links to a requirement, test, and evidence ID.
- Applicability and exclusions have documented rationale.
- Risk acceptance has named approval and review/expiry date.
- Actions have owners, deadlines, and objective closure evidence.
- A second reviewer can reproduce the results.

## Evidence and reporting checklist
- [ ] Approved charter, scope, and roles
- [ ] Requirements register and source editions
- [ ] Control owners and risk owners
- [ ] Test plan, sampling rationale, and dated results
- [ ] Evidence index with source, owner, date, and integrity notes
- [ ] Findings and business impact
- [ ] Treatment decisions, exceptions, approvals, and due dates
- [ ] Executive report and limitations
