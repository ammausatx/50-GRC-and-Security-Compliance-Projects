# Project 09: Create a Vendor and Third-Party Inventory

**Level:** Beginner | **Track:** GRC & Security Compliance

## Objective
Build and execute a repeatable create a vendor and third-party inventory exercise for a fictional organization. Demonstrate accountable governance, risk-informed decisions, traceable requirements, and evidence-based conclusions.

## Scope and authorization
Use synthetic records and fictional business units only. Define entities, services, systems, data, suppliers, exclusions, assumptions, and assessment dates. Do not place real personal, regulated, customer, or confidential information in this public training repository. This is not legal advice, certification, or an audit opinion.

## Prerequisites
- Spreadsheet or GRC workspace; document editor; diagramming tool
- Fictional sponsor, process owner, control owner, reviewer, and risk approver
- Official source material for the applicable framework and its edition
- Synthetic sample data with unique identifiers

## Lab setup
1. Create folders `01-scope`, `02-requirements`, `03-assessment`, `04-evidence`, `05-remediation`, and `06-report`.
2. Record purpose, scope boundary, stakeholders, systems, data types, suppliers, exclusions, and assumptions.
3. Define unique IDs for requirements, controls, risks, evidence, findings, and actions.
4. Agree on status values, severity criteria, sampling method, and approval workflow.
5. Version all artifacts and record the assessment date and source editions.

## Walkthrough
### 1. Establish context
Draft a project charter explaining the business objective, in-scope process, relevant stakeholders, dependencies, roles, and exclusions. Obtain sponsor approval.

### 2. Translate requirements into criteria
Create a traceable register. For every applicable criterion record source and exact reference, applicability rationale, expected outcome, accountable owner, test procedure, and evidence required. Distinguish legal/contractual obligations from voluntary guidance.

### 3. Build the assessment register
Include ID, requirement, owner, control description, design status, operating status, evidence ID, test date, result, gap, risk rating, treatment, due date, and reviewer. Define scoring scales before using them.

### 4. Perform a repeatable review
Interview the fictional owner, inspect synthetic policies/configuration/process records, and sample representative items. Record sample selection, population, procedure, observed facts, exceptions, limitations, and evidence location. Mark unsupported assertions as unable to verify.

### 5. Analyze gaps and risk
Compare design and operation against the criteria. Document condition, expected state, root cause, business impact, likelihood, severity, affected scope, and confidence. Do not treat a written policy as proof that a control operates.

### 6. Decide and remediate
Select mitigation, avoidance, transfer/share, or documented acceptance. Assign an accountable owner, milestones, resources, interim safeguards, target date, and residual-risk approver. Exceptions require rationale, compensating controls, expiry, and review date.

### 7. Report and retain
Prepare an executive summary, scope and methodology, evidence index, results, prioritized actions, management decisions, limitations, and next review date. Keep sensitive source evidence in an appropriately protected system; publish only synthetic examples.

## Tools and checks
Use spreadsheet validation, unique-ID checks, filters, pivot summaries, document version history, approval records, and a diagram of scope and responsibilities. Optional scripts can flag duplicate IDs, missing owners, overdue actions, and broken evidence references. Use read-only synthetic fixtures; production testing requires written authorization.

## Expected output
A reviewable assessment pack: approved charter, criteria register, test records, evidence index, risk/gap log, treatment plan, approvals, and executive report. Every conclusion must trace to a criterion and a recorded test.

## Findings and risk assessment
Classify results as effective, partially effective, ineffective, not implemented, not applicable with rationale, or unable to verify. Prioritize using business impact, likelihood, exposure, and regulatory/contractual relevance. Separate control design from operating effectiveness.

## Framework mapping
Select the applicable framework and record its exact edition and reference IDs. Depending on scope, useful crosswalks may include ISO/IEC 27001:2022, NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, CIS Controls v8, SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria, and relevant sector rules. Crosswalks are informative; mapped controls do not automatically satisfy every requirement.

## Remediation
For each confirmed gap record root cause, corrective action, owner, priority, target date, dependencies, interim safeguards, required closure evidence, and independent validation. Escalate overdue high-risk actions through governance.

## Validation criteria
- Approved and versioned scope, criteria, and methodology
- Each conclusion links to a requirement, test, and evidence ID
- Applicability decisions and exclusions have rationale
- Risk acceptance has accountable approval and expiry/review date
- Corrective actions have owners, deadlines, and closure evidence
- An independent reviewer can reproduce the assessment

## Evidence and reporting checklist
- [ ] Approved charter and scope boundary
- [ ] Requirements register with source editions
- [ ] Named control and risk owners
- [ ] Test plan, sample selection, and dated results
- [ ] Evidence index with ID, source, owner, date, and integrity notes
- [ ] Findings with impact and supporting facts
- [ ] Treatment plan, exceptions, approvals, and due dates
- [ ] Executive summary and limitations statement
