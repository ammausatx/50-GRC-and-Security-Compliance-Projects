# Project 33: Business Impact Analysis and RTO RPO Workshop

**Level:** Intermediate | **Track:** GRC & Security Compliance

## Objective
Design, operate, and evaluate a business impact analysis and rto rpo workshop capability in a fictional enterprise. Produce measurable outcomes, accountable decisions, traceable evidence, and a sustainable improvement cycle.

## Scope and authorization
Use synthetic data and fictional stakeholders. Define organization, services, systems, data, suppliers, boundaries, exclusions, assumptions, and review period. Protect real personal, regulated, customer, and confidential information. This project is educational, not legal advice, certification, or an audit opinion.

## Prerequisites
- Current official requirements and relevant framework edition
- Defined governance roles and approved scope
- Spreadsheet/GRC platform and controlled document repository
- Synthetic evidence records, sample events, and process documentation

## Lab setup
1. Create charter, scope, criteria, control, evidence, risk, action, and reporting folders.
2. Assign unique identifiers and controlled status values.
3. Define ownership, review frequency, risk scoring, evidence standards, and escalation.
4. Establish a baseline period and synthetic dataset.
5. Record source editions, assumptions, and limitations.

## Walkthrough
### 1. Establish objectives and scope
Describe the business objective, stakeholders, services, dependencies, data types, system boundaries, exclusions, and decision authority. Obtain sponsor approval.

### 2. Define measurable criteria
Convert requirements and objectives into testable criteria. Record source, reference, applicability, target state, metric or test, owner, evidence source, and review cadence.

### 3. Establish operating model
Document roles, workflow, approvals, segregation of duties, escalation, exceptions, and reporting. Ensure every control or metric has an accountable owner and a defined audience.

### 4. Collect and validate evidence
Use synthetic records to test the process. Define population and sample method, capture dated test results, record evidence IDs, and validate source integrity. Distinguish missing records from failed controls.

### 5. Analyze results and trends
Compare results to criteria and baseline. Identify recurring issues, overdue actions, concentration of risk, exceptions, and dependencies. Record data quality limitations and avoid unsupported precision.

### 6. Decide and improve
Prioritize actions based on business impact and likelihood. Assign owners, target dates, interim safeguards, and closure evidence. Require approval and expiry for accepted risk or exceptions.

### 7. Communicate and review
Create management reporting with trends, thresholds, significant exceptions, decisions required, action status, and next review. Establish a periodic feedback loop to refine the process and measures.

## Tools and checks
Use a GRC register, spreadsheet formulas, pivot summaries, trend charts, evidence index, and issue tracker. Validate denominators, reporting period, source, owner, calculation, thresholds, and stale data. Keep metric definitions stable across reporting periods.

## Expected output
An approved operating process, criteria and ownership register, synthetic evidence pack, results analysis, risk/action log, management dashboard or report, and recurring review schedule.

## Findings and risk assessment
Explain the significance of each result, scope and data limitations, recurrence, affected services, likelihood, impact, and dependencies. Avoid treating a metric target as proof of control effectiveness without test evidence.

## Framework mapping
Use applicable references from ISO/IEC 27001:2022, NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, CIS Controls v8, SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria, or relevant regulatory obligations. Record exact editions and explain how each reference informs the criterion; do not claim automatic equivalence.

## Remediation
Track root cause, owner, priority, milestones, due date, interim safeguards, dependencies, closure evidence, and independent validation. Escalate material overdue actions to the governance body.

## Validation criteria
- Objectives, scope, and accountability approved
- Criteria and measures have definitions and sources
- Evidence is dated, attributable, and traceable
- Metrics have validated formulas and denominators
- Exceptions and risk decisions have approval and expiry
- Actions have owners and independently verified closure
- Reporting includes trends, limitations, and decisions

## Evidence and reporting checklist
- [ ] Charter, scope, assumptions, and approvals
