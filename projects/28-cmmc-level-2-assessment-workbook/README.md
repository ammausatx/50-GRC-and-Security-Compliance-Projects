# Project 28: CMMC Level 2 Assessment Workbook

**Level:** Intermediate | **Track:** GRC & Security Compliance

## Objective
Build a practical cmmc level 2 assessment workbook capability for a fictional organization and demonstrate how governance requirements become assigned, measured, and reviewable activities.

## Scope and authorization
Use fictional business units and synthetic records. Establish the boundary, services, systems, data, suppliers, exclusions, assumptions, and assessment period. Keep real sensitive evidence out of this public repository. The exercise is educational and does not provide legal advice, certification, or an audit opinion.

## Prerequisites
- Current official source documents and applicable framework edition
- Named sponsor, process owner, control owner, reviewer, and risk approver
- Spreadsheet/GRC workspace, document repository, and diagramming tool
- Synthetic sample records, policies, tickets, and registers

## Lab setup
1. Create a charter and scope boundary.
2. Create linked registers for requirements, controls, risks, evidence, findings, and actions.
3. Assign unique IDs, data owners, reviewers, and status definitions.
4. Define assessment methodology, scoring, evidence quality, sampling, and approval rules.
5. Set a review cadence and version-controlled repository.

## Walkthrough
### 1. Define the business context
Describe objectives, critical services, stakeholders, information assets, dependencies, and applicable obligations. Record in-scope and excluded entities with rationale.

### 2. Establish the criteria
Translate the subject into measurable criteria with source, exact reference, applicability, expected result, owner, evidence source, and test method. Confirm authoritative requirements from official publications.

### 3. Design the process and controls
Document workflow steps, decision points, responsibilities, segregation of duties, approvals, exception handling, monitoring, and escalation. Identify preventive, detective, and corrective measures.

### 4. Execute the review
Use synthetic evidence to inspect design and operation. Define the population and sample method, retain sample IDs, record test steps, and note exceptions. Capture date, tester, reviewer, and limitations for each workpaper.

### 5. Evaluate performance and exposure
Compare results to criteria. Record gaps, root causes, impact, likelihood, severity, control dependencies, and residual exposure. Distinguish control design from consistent operation.

### 6. Assign decisions and actions
Prioritize issues and assign accountable owners, milestones, due dates, interim safeguards, dependencies, and closure evidence. Risk acceptance requires documented rationale, approval authority, expiry, and reassessment.

### 7. Report and improve
Create an executive summary, methodology, coverage, exceptions, risk trends, action status, decisions needed, and next review date. Retain an audit trail for changes and approvals.

## Tools and checks
Use spreadsheet validation, duplicate-ID detection, completeness checks, filters, pivot summaries, and overdue-action tracking. Preserve version history, reviewer comments, approvals, and evidence references. Keep official source material separate from internal interpretations.

## Expected output
A repeatable operating procedure, scoped criteria, ownership matrix, control/test register, synthetic evidence pack, issue and risk log, action plan, and management report.

## Findings and risk assessment
Rate findings using consistent likelihood and impact definitions. Consider scope, recurrence, business criticality, dependencies, evidence reliability, and compensating controls. Record uncertainty and do not mark a requirement satisfied without support.

## Framework mapping
Map relevant criteria to exact editions and control references. Potential reference points include ISO/IEC 27001:2022, NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, CIS Controls v8, and applicable regulatory or contractual obligations. Mapping is a navigation aid, not proof of equivalence.

## Remediation
Record root cause, corrective action, accountable owner, priority, milestones, due date, interim safeguard, required closure evidence, and retest approach. Escalate overdue high-risk actions through the governance forum.

## Validation criteria
- Approved scope, process, and responsibilities
- Criteria linked to authoritative sources
- Complete and versioned registers with unique IDs
- Evidence and tests are dated, attributable, and reproducible
- Exceptions and risk decisions have appropriate approval
- Actions have owners, due dates, and objective closure evidence
- Management receives status and limitations

## Evidence and reporting checklist
- [ ] Charter, scope, assumptions, and exclusions
- [ ] Source register and criteria mapping
- [ ] Process documentation and ownership
- [ ] Test plan, population, samples, and results
- [ ] Evidence index and approval trail
- [ ] Findings, risk ratings, and root causes
- [ ] Corrective actions and accepted-risk records
- [ ] Executive report and review cadence

## Completion criteria
