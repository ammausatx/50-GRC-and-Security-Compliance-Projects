# Project 03: Create an Asset Inventory and Ownership Register

**Level:** Beginner  
**Track:** Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)

## Objective
Design and execute a practical create an asset inventory and ownership register exercise for a fictional organization. Produce traceable governance artifacts that demonstrate how requirements are translated into accountable controls, measurable risk decisions, and verifiable evidence.

## Scope and authorization
Use a fictional company and synthetic records only. Define organizational boundaries, systems, business owners, data types, locations, and relevant suppliers before assessing them. Do not upload real personal, regulated, customer, or confidential data. This exercise is not a certification, legal opinion, or independent audit.

## Prerequisites
- Spreadsheet or GRC workspace for registers and evidence tracking
- Document editor and diagramming tool
- A named project sponsor, control owner, reviewer, and approval authority (fictional roles are acceptable)
- Applicable framework or regulatory source material obtained from its official publisher
- A small synthetic dataset with unique IDs and no real secrets

## Lab setup
1. Create a workspace with folders: `01-scope`, `02-requirements`, `03-assessment`, `04-evidence`, `05-remediation`, and `06-report`.
2. Create a scope record with business purpose, entities, services, systems, exclusions, assumptions, and review date.
3. Assign unique IDs to requirements, risks, controls, evidence items, findings, and actions.
4. Use fictional roles and sample values; keep evidence versioned and access-controlled.
5. Record the framework edition and assessment date so results can be reproduced.

## Walkthrough
### Step 1 — Establish context
Write a one-page project charter. Identify the business objective, stakeholders, in-scope processes, assets, data, third parties, dependencies, and exclusions. Obtain documented sponsor approval.

### Step 2 — Define criteria
Translate the project topic into testable criteria. For each criterion record its source, exact reference, applicability rationale, expected outcome, owner, and evidence needed. Distinguish mandatory obligations from voluntary good practice.

### Step 3 — Build the working register
Create a structured register with, at minimum: ID, description, category, owner, inherent exposure, current safeguards, evidence reference, assessment status, residual exposure, decision, due date, and reviewer. Use consistent status values and scoring definitions.

### Step 4 — Assess and test
Interview the fictional owner, inspect synthetic configuration or process records, and sample representative entries. Record what was examined, sample size, test procedure, result, limitations, and evidence location. Mark unsupported claims as unverified rather than compliant.

### Step 5 — Evaluate gaps and risk
Compare observed design and operation with each criterion. Document the condition, expected requirement, cause, business impact, likelihood, severity, and affected scope. Separate a missing control from a control that exists but is not operating effectively.

### Step 6 — Treat and approve
For each material gap, select a documented treatment: mitigate, avoid, transfer/share, or accept. Assign an accountable owner, milestones, target date, resources, and residual-risk approver. Exceptions must have rationale, compensating safeguards, expiry, and review date.

### Step 7 — Report and retain
Prepare an executive summary, scope statement, methodology, results, prioritized actions, limitations, and management decisions. Store evidence references and approvals in the project workspace; do not expose sensitive evidence in a public repository.

## Tools and example checks
- Spreadsheet: filters, validation lists, pivot summaries, and unique IDs
- Document repository: version history, reviewer comments, approval records
- Diagramming: scope boundary, process flow, and responsibility model
- Optional scripts: validate required fields, duplicate IDs, overdue actions, and missing evidence links
- Use read-only exports or synthetic fixtures; never test production controls without written authorization.

## Expected output
A scoped and reviewable assessment pack containing a charter, criteria register, assessment records, evidence index, risk/gap log, treatment plan, approvals, and an executive report. Every conclusion should trace to a requirement and a recorded test.

## Findings and risk assessment
Classify observations as effective, partially effective, ineffective, not implemented, not applicable (with rationale), or unable to verify. Prioritize by business impact, likelihood, exposure, and regulatory or contractual relevance. Do not equate a policy document with an operating control.

## Framework mapping
Use the framework relevant to the subject and document exact edition and control references. Common crosswalks may include ISO/IEC 27001:2022, NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, CIS Controls v8, SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria, and applicable sector requirements. Mappings are informative; one framework's control does not automatically satisfy another framework's requirement.

## Remediation
Create a corrective-action record for every confirmed gap. Include root cause, action description, owner, priority, target date, dependencies, interim safeguards, required evidence, and independent validation. Escalate overdue high-risk items through the defined governance forum.

## Validation criteria
- Scope and assessment criteria are approved and versioned.
- Each conclusion links to a requirement, test, and evidence reference.
- Applicability and exclusions include documented rationale.
- Risk decisions have accountable approval and review dates.
- Corrective actions have owners, due dates, and closure evidence.
- A reviewer can reproduce the assessment from the retained records.

## Evidence and reporting checklist
- [ ] Approved charter and scope boundary
- [ ] Requirements / criteria register with source editions
- [ ] Named control and risk owners
- [ ] Test plan, sample selection, and dated test results
- [ ] Evidence index with IDs, source, owner, date, and integrity notes
- [ ] Findings with impact and supporting facts
- [ ] Treatment plan, exceptions, approvals, and target dates
- [ ] Executive summary and limitations statement
